Relationship terms can overlap, and people can use the same term differently. Consent distinguishes **consensual non-monogamy** from infidelity. Polygamy describes plural marriage, while polyamory describes consensual openness to multiple loving or romantic relationships. The definitions below are based on an [academic overview of consensual non-monogamy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25189189/), the [Planned Parenthood Toronto glossary](https://ppt.on.ca/factsheets/consensual-non-monogamy/), the [Ohio State University LGBTQ+ glossary](https://u.osu.edu/lgbtqhealthequity/lgbtq-language-glossary-resources/), and [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/polygamy). These are general terms and are not necessarily current OkCupid profile options.

| Relationship type | Category | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monogamous | Exclusivity agreement | The participants agree to have one romantic or sexual partnership at a time. The exact boundaries depend on their agreement. |
| Serial monogamy | Exclusivity pattern | A person has a sequence of monogamous relationships, with no intended overlap between them. |
| Consensual non-monogamy (CNM) or ethical non-monogamy (ENM) | Umbrella term | Everyone involved knowingly agrees that participants can have additional sexual, romantic, or intimate connections. |
| Open relationship | CNM agreement | Partners agree that one or more of them can have sexual, and sometimes romantic, connections outside the existing relationship. |
| Monogamish | CNM agreement | A mostly monogamous relationship that permits limited or occasional consensual sexual activity with other people. |
| Swinging | CNM agreement | Partners, often an established couple, engage in consensual recreational sexual activity with other people, commonly together or in a social setting. Romantic bonds are usually not the focus. |
| Polyamorous | CNM agreement | The participants are open to multiple concurrent loving or romantic relationships, with the knowledge and consent of everyone involved. |
| Hierarchical polyamory | Polyamory structure | Some relationships have explicitly greater priority, rights, or commitments than others. Terms such as `primary` and `secondary` can describe the levels. |
| Non-hierarchical polyamory | Polyamory structure | The participants do not formally rank relationships. Each relationship has its own commitments and agreements. |
| Solo polyamory | Polyamory structure | A person maintains multiple relationships while prioritizing personal autonomy and without assuming a primary partner, cohabitation, or merged finances. |
| Polyfidelity | Closed CNM agreement | Three or more people form a closed relationship and agree not to pursue sexual or romantic connections outside the group. |
| Relationship anarchy (RA) | Relationship framework | Each relationship develops through its own agreements instead of default social labels or hierarchies. RA can be monogamous or non-monogamous. |
| Closed relationship | Availability agreement | The participants do not accept new sexual or romantic partners outside the agreed relationship. A closed relationship can contain two or more people. |
| Polygamous | Plural marriage | One person is married to more than one spouse at the same time. Polygamy is not the same as polyamory. |
| Polygyny | Plural marriage | One husband is married to more than one wife at the same time. |
| Polyandry | Plural marriage | One wife is married to more than one husband at the same time. |
| Mono-poly relationship | Mixed agreement | One participant remains monogamous while another consensually maintains or can form multiple relationships. The agreement must define what each person can do. |
| Triad or throuple | Multi-partner configuration | Three people are each romantically or sexually connected to both of the other people. |
| Quad | Multi-partner configuration | Four people form an interconnected relationship network. Not every person must have the same type of connection with every other person. |
| V or vee | Multi-partner configuration | One person, called the hinge, has separate relationships with two people who are not in a relationship with each other. |
| Polycule | Relationship network | The complete network of people connected through polyamorous relationships. Not every member dates every other member. |
| Kitchen-table polyamory | Interaction style | Partners and metamours, meaning a partner’s other partners, are comfortable interacting socially, such as sharing a meal together. |
| Parallel polyamory | Interaction style | A person’s separate relationships coexist with little or no direct interaction between their partners. |
